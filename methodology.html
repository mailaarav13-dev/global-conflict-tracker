GLOBAL CONFLICT TRACKER

Methodology and limitations

This tracker combines an editorial conflict catalog with frequently refreshed links to reporting. It is an orientation tool, not an independent verification service or an emergency alert system.

What is updated automatically

News discovery is refreshed on a scheduled background workflow. GDELT supplies multilingual article discovery; NewsAPI can be enabled as an additional provider using a server-side credential. Results are filtered by conflict-specific terms, deduplicated by normalized URL and headline, validated, and saved as a static snapshot.

What is editorial

Conflict titles, summaries, status labels, historical context, strategic impact, and country associations are maintained in the editorial conflict catalog. They do not change merely because a new headline appears.

Freshness and failures

Every country panel shows the snapshot’s update date. If a provider is unavailable or rate-limits a request, the pipeline retains the last valid articles. The interface labels a snapshot when the last successful update is more than 48 hours old.

Important limitations

  • Inclusion of a headline does not confirm that every claim in the linked article is accurate.
  • Reported casualty figures and territorial claims can change or conflict across sources.
  • Automated keyword matching can omit relevant reporting or occasionally include a marginal result.
  • The absence of a tracked situation does not mean a country is free of political violence or humanitarian risk.

Source transparency

News entries link directly to the original publisher and display the provider and publication date when available. Background citations appear in each conflict profile. Planned structured-data additions include ACLED or UCDP event data and ReliefWeb, HDX, and UNHCR humanitarian indicators, each with its own coverage period and methodology label.
